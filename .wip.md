## SSD recovery (`/dev/sda1`, "Usb_stick", 1.8TB FAT32, corrupted)

Read-only `fsck.vfat -n -v` dry-run run 2026-09-01, 16:42-20:21 (~4h).
**Real progress this time** (unlike the earlier 9.5h/0.18%-read stall):
confirmed FAT table structurally intact, found several files with
corrupted cluster chains (0-byte size, nonzero chain length - would
truncate in write mode), hit at least one raw sector I/O error. Did NOT
finish cleanly - kernel logged a fresh "not properly unmounted" mount
event at the exact log-stop timestamp, meaning the drive most likely
disconnected/reset at the USB level under sustained load, not a clean
completion. Matches the drive's already-documented pattern of real
hardware degradation under sustained I/O (see `project_backup_system`
memory for the full history). Drive safely unmounted + powered off via
`udisksctl` this session, confirmed gone from `lsblk` - safe to
physically remove, log preserved at
`/home/rapi5/Documents/Scripts/fsck_dryrun.log`.

**Real next step, not decided yet**: given TWO dry-run attempts now show
the drive struggling under sustained read load (this one for ~4h before
disconnecting, the earlier one for 9.5h reading only 3.3GB), a full
file-level fsck/rsync pass may not be realistic. Worth deciding: try
`ddrescue` instead (built specifically for failing drives - skips bad
sectors, retries them last, instead of fsck's assume-healthy-media
approach), or accept that only the confirmed sole-copy content (Movies
502GB + TV Series 115GB, already reclaimed NVMe space waiting for it,
see `project_backup_system` memory) is worth fighting for rather than a
full-drive recovery.
